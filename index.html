Chris Penner

Solution architect professionally. Maker and occasional vibe coder for fun.

I became a technologist more or less by accident and have now spent two decades at it, across tech and government, mostly designing systems and being very confident about my boxes and arrows. In my own time I build the things instead, which is a useful corrective. Nearly everything below started as something I wanted to exist, could not buy, and was too stubborn to drop.

I am dyslexic, loudly and unrepentantly. It shows up in my work in ways both useful — I tend to see the shape of a problem well before I can spell it — and less useful, for which see the domain name below. Mostly it has left me interested in tools that carry some of the load rather than handing all of it back to you.

Things I've made

NoteTaker

Android · Kotlin · coming to Google Play

Records a meeting, or takes an MP3 you feed it, and emails you back a transcript and a summary. It separates the speakers, and there is a context field so the model knows whether it is sitting in an engineering review or a legal one. The whole thing runs on the handset — your own API key, your own mail account, no server of mine in the middle. Privacy policy.

Forked: The Workplace Advantages of the Neurodivergent Mind

Book · Kindle

I mentor technical staff, and somewhere around the fourth time I gave the same advice I concluded it would be more efficient to write it down. It argues that a neurodivergent brain is not a deficit in need of patching but a different operating system — and that it is usually the workplace, not the person, that wants refactoring. The writing is on the wall, and it is badly spelled.

Grimoire Combat Helper

dandmonsters.com

A monster compendium and combat tracker built for my kid, with Fable 5 on words and Google's image generator on beasts. It is also standing proof that dyslexics should not be left unsupervised with a domain registrar: I was going for D&D Monsters. Read it again.

Côtes du Phone

cotesduphone.co.uk

A phone holder that sits over the neck of a wine bottle, so you can watch something at dinner without folding yourself into the shape of a question mark. Self-taught 3D design and printing, taken all the way to a real product that real people have actually paid for. The pun arrived some time before the product did.
